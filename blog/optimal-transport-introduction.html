Introduction to Optimal Transport Theory

March 15, 2024 By Abdelwahed Khamis

What is Optimal Transport?

Optimal Transport (OT) is a mathematical framework that provides a way to compare probability distributions by finding the most efficient way to transform one distribution into another. The concept was first introduced by Gaspard Monge in 1781 and later developed by Leonid Kantorovich in the 1940s.

Figure 1: Visual representation of optimal transport between two distributions

The Mathematical Formulation

Given two probability measures \(\mu\) and \(\nu\) on spaces \(X\) and \(Y\) respectively, the optimal transport problem seeks to find a transport plan \(\pi\) that minimizes the total cost of moving mass from \(\mu\) to \(\nu\). The problem can be formulated as:

\[ \inf_{\pi \in \Pi(\mu, \nu)} \int_{X \times Y} \color{blue}{c(x,y)} \color{red}{d\pi(x,y)} \]
Equation 1: The optimal transport problem formulation

where \(\Pi(\mu, \nu)\) is the set of all transport plans between \(\mu\) and \(\nu\), and \(\color{blue}{c(x,y)}\) is the cost function.

Figure 2: Example of a transport plan between discrete distributions

The Wasserstein Distance

When the cost function is a metric, the optimal transport problem defines a distance between probability measures known as the Wasserstein distance (or Earth Mover's Distance):

\[ W_p(\mu, \nu) = \left( \inf_{\pi \in \Pi(\mu, \nu)} \int_{X \times Y} \color{green}{d(x,y)^p} \color{red}{d\pi(x,y)} \right)^{1/p} \]
Equation 2: The Wasserstein distance definition

Applications in Machine Learning

Optimal Transport has found numerous applications in machine learning, including:

  • Generative models (GANs, WGAN)
  • Domain adaptation
  • Clustering and classification
  • Natural language processing

Example: Computing the Wasserstein Distance

Consider two discrete distributions \(\mu\) and \(\nu\) with support points \(x_1, x_2\) and \(y_1, y_2\) respectively. The optimal transport problem becomes a linear programming problem:

\[ \begin{align} \min_{P} & \sum_{i,j} \color{blue}{c_{ij}} \color{red}{p_{ij}} \\ \text{s.t.} & \sum_j \color{red}{p_{ij}} = \color{green}{\mu_i} \\ & \sum_i \color{red}{p_{ij}} = \color{green}{\nu_j} \\ & \color{red}{p_{ij}} \geq 0 \end{align} \]
Equation 3: The linear programming formulation of the optimal transport problem

Conclusion

Optimal Transport provides a powerful framework for comparing and transforming probability distributions. Its applications in machine learning and data science continue to grow, making it an essential tool in modern data analysis.
